# Historical Minnan transcription

This is a deliverable draft, not an installed skill. Its empirically tested component is narrow: learning seven published DHS tone-symbol examples and applying the coding method to a few manuscript instances. It is not a complete historical-to-modern conversion system.

## Produce two separate layers
- Main text: diplomatic transcription, source page/row anchors, necessary uncertainty. Preserve historical glyphs, abbreviations, tone marks, spelling variants, deletions and additions in distinguishable form.
- Supporting research: proposed corrections, scholarly codes, phonetic interpretations, modern comparisons, calibration evidence and work logs. Never replace the original layer with a plausible modern reading.

Use additive versions and source hashes for this project. Do not overwrite frozen manuscripts or equate line-mapping counts with corrected-error counts.

## Learn the actual notation before interpreting it
For DHS tone symbols, read [the seven-example learning sheet](../DHS_seven_tone_symbols_learning_sheet_v1.md) and inspect its linked images. Five ordinary codes and two checked-tone composite codes are distinct. A handwritten composite can resemble a cross or hook, rather than two separated printed strokes.

Record three fields:
1. Observable stroke structure and uncertainty: principal diagonal, secondary branch, attachment and position relative to the syllable.
2. Published transcription code, explicitly identifying the author's convention and alternatives.
3. Proposed phonetic/tone-class interpretation, citing its source and confidence.

A generic × or circumflex description alone does not establish the author's code. Conversely, code agreement does not prove a reconstructed pitch value. Do not assign a modern tone solely from the Chinese character.

For letters, separate genuine strokes from tone marks, underlines and neighbouring letters. Use same-hand examples and adjacent rows. Retain c/g, t/r, i/y and inserted-u differences as written; interpreting them as equivalent sounds is a later hypothesis. A scholar's high-frequency preferred spelling is not permission to normalize a rarer original.

## Use multilingual evidence constructively
First preserve an independent reading. Then cross-check Spanish headword meaning, Chinese lexical item and Minnan annotation, adding Mandarin only where that column really exists. Use historical Spanish senses and relevant regional/literary-colloquial alternatives. Semantic fit supports a candidate; it does not make an invisible stroke certain.

DHS, DSH, UST tomo214 Vocabulario, Arte and HAB are distinct sources. Match sense, context and bibliographical identity before aligning entries. A shorter dictionary's missing entry is not a contradiction. Continuous religious prose needs parallel passages and terminology rather than an assumed dictionary-style one-to-one alignment.

## Interpret historical and modern language in layers
Read [the evidence synthesis](../DHS_historical_to_modern_minnan_layers_v1.md) when doing interpretation. Distinguish Spanish sound change, Spanish spelling conventions, custom missionary notation, Minnan dialect/literary-colloquial variation and proposed Minnan sound change. Do not silently read old j/x/y or final r by modern Spanish defaults. Do not force all Minnan into Taiwanese Tâi-lô or a single regional pronunciation.

Modern intelligibility is valuable per-entry evidence of continuity. It does not establish unchanged pitch, sandhi or all sound values over four centuries. Label region and citation, and separate citation tone from contextual tone.

## Check sources and images
Locate each paper crop in the full manuscript using surrounding rows and distinctive corrections, then record both PDF and printed page. Offsets are not globally constant. Similarity scores are only candidates: earlier small-scale searches produced strong false positives. Full-page and local visual checks are required before calling a match confirmed.

If a paper labels a reading as an error, record its criterion. Grady's majority/minority spelling classifications and some phonetic explanations are interpretive; they are not infallible visual answers. Mark author-stated rules, inference from examples and project proposals separately.

## Calibrate without overstating results
Give a reader only authorized teaching examples and test images, never reference keys. Freeze the reader's text/hash before comparison. Distinguish project-wide fresh material from historical material new only to that reader; disclose contextual isolation versus actual file-permission isolation.

Predeclare scored fields. Report primary agreement with residual alternatives separately from unqualified agreement; an answer appearing only among alternatives is not a full success. Uncovered tone/letter fields stay unscored. Different readers or sample difficulty prevent causal claims about teaching improvement.
